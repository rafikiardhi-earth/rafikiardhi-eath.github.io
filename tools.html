Profile harmoniser

Fit a physics-informed spline (phySpline) to irregular horizon data and harmonise it to standard depth intervals. Works for continuous properties (carbon, pH, clay) and for ordered classes such as texture.

1. Your data

Columns: profile id, top depth (cm), bottom depth (cm), then one or more property columns. Comma, semicolon or tab separated. Up to 20,000 rows.

Harmonised values

Intervals that extend past the bottom of a profile are averaged over the soil that is present and shown in amber. Blank means no soil in that interval.

Soil carbon stocks

Calculate organic carbon stocks for many profiles at once, by summing horizons to fixed depths, with an optional equivalent soil mass basis for comparing sites or sampling dates.

1. Your data

Columns: id, top (cm), bottom (cm), soc (%), bd (bulk density, g/cm³), and optionally cf (coarse fragments, % by volume).

2. Settings

Equivalent soil mass: stock in the soil needed to reach the reference mass, regardless of depth. Useful when bulk density differs between sites or dates.

Stocks (Mg C/ha)

Amber values: the profile is shallower than the reporting depth, so the stock covers only the soil that was sampled.

Horizon sums can overestimate stocks

Summing horizons assumes each layer is uniform. Our published work shows this can substantially overestimate carbon stocks compared with assessing the profile at every point. For baselines, credits or reporting, we calculate stocks from depth-continuous profiles with uncertainty.

Model evaluation

Columns named obs and pred. Add sd (prediction standard deviation) for a coverage curve, or lower and upper for the coverage of your own intervals.

Confusion matrix

Rows are observed classes, columns are predicted. Producer's accuracy: share of each observed class predicted correctly. User's accuracy: share of each predicted class that is correct.

Class probabilities

First column is the site id, then one column of probability per class.

CI = 1 − (P1 − P2), where P1 and P2 are the two highest class probabilities. 0 means one class clearly wins; 1 means the top two are tied.

Need uncertainty for a whole map?

We build bootstrapped maps with prediction intervals, confusion indices and independent validation, so users know where a map can be trusted and where it can't.

Pedodiversity

Measure the taxonomic diversity of soils in an area: how many soil classes there are, and how evenly the area is shared between them. Compare several sites at once.

Your soil classes

A column named class is required (any classification: soil orders, reference soil groups, series, map units). Add area or count to weight each row; without one, every row counts once (for example, one row per profile). Add site to compare several areas.

Indices by site

Indices

Share of each class

Reading the indices

Richness counts classes. Shannon (H′) rises with both more classes and more even shares. Evenness (J′) is Shannon relative to its maximum for that many classes, from 0 (one class dominates) to 1 (all equal). Simpson is the chance that two random points fall in different classes.

Hill numbers give the "effective number of classes": N1 weights classes by their share, N2 counts only the common ones. Dominance is the share of the most common class.

Diversity is more than counting classes

Taxonomic indices treat every class as equally different and ignore where soils sit and how they change with depth. Our approach measures pedodiversity from continuous soil properties in three dimensions, as the organisation of energy across the soil system.
