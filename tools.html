Tools

Free tools treat the soil horizon by horizon. Our advanced methods treat it as one connected system, continuous with depth and across the landscape, because that is how water, carbon and energy actually move.

Everything runs in your browser. Uploaded data never leaves your device.

Profile harmoniser

Fit a physics-informed spline (phySpline) to irregular horizon data and harmonise it to standard depth intervals. Works for continuous properties (carbon, pH, clay) and for ordered classes such as texture.

1. Your data

Columns: profile id, top depth (cm), bottom depth (cm), then one or more property columns. Comma, semicolon or tab separated. Up to 20,000 rows.

2. Settings

Profile view

Observed horizons Spline Harmonised intervals

Harmonised values

Intervals that extend past the bottom of a profile are averaged over the soil that is present and shown in amber. Blank means no soil in that interval.

Need more than horizon-based harmonisation?

Our holomorphic spline treats the profile as one continuous system: much faster on large datasets, with uncertainty at every depth and boundary-aware profiles linked to water and heat flux.

Soil carbon stocks

Calculate organic carbon stocks for many profiles at once, by summing horizons to fixed depths, with an optional equivalent soil mass basis for comparing sites or sampling dates.

1. Your data

Columns: id, top (cm), bottom (cm), soc (%), bd (bulk density, g/cm³), and optionally cf (coarse fragments, % by volume).

2. Settings

Equivalent soil mass: stock in the soil needed to reach the reference mass, regardless of depth. Useful when bulk density differs between sites or dates.

Stocks (Mg C/ha)

Amber values: the profile is shallower than the reporting depth, so the stock covers only the soil that was sampled.

Horizon sums can overestimate stocks

Summing horizons assumes each layer is uniform. Our published work shows this can substantially overestimate carbon stocks compared with assessing the profile at every point. For baselines, credits or reporting, we calculate stocks from depth-continuous profiles with uncertainty.

Munsell soil colour

See a Munsell notation as an on-screen colour, browse a soil chart page, and learn what the colour usually tells you about the soil.

Notation

–

Chart page

Rows are value (lightness), columns are chroma (strength). Tap a chip to select it.

Colours are computed from the Munsell renotation data (illuminant C) and converted to sRGB. Screens vary, so use this for learning, not in place of a physical chart. Chips that fall outside the sRGB range are left out.

Sampling density

Estimate how many samples you need to measure a field or site mean within a chosen margin of error, assuming simple random sampling.

Inputs

No CV to hand? Typical values: pH 5–15%, organic carbon 20–40%, available P or K 40–80%.

Spatial variation changes the answer

Random sampling ignores the fact that nearby soils are alike. We design optimised and stratified sampling schemes and management zones that reach the same precision with fewer samples.
