Soil Colour Reader

Munsell soil colour from a photo, with uncertainty

Estimate the Munsell soil colour of a matrix from a photo, with optional grey or black-and-white card correction and probabilities over neighbouring chips.
NoteNo AI. No machine learning.

The colour is worked out with published colour science, step by step: the photo is corrected against your reference card, converted to CIELAB, and compared with the Munsell soil-book chips computed from the Munsell renotation data. The uncertainty is calculated from the pixels you sample and the stated error of each card method. The same photo with the circles in the same place always gives the same answer. Nothing is trained on other people’s photos.

Place the circles on the soil matrix and on your reference card, then read the result. Load a photo from your phone camera or gallery; the sample photo shows a 10YR 4/3 matrix under a warm colour cast.

    How the estimate is made

    Pixels inside the soil circle are decoded to linear RGB. Clipped highlights and very dark pixels (voids, deep shadow) are dropped, and the brightest and darkest 10% are trimmed. With a black and white card each channel is mapped with a two-point line through the card patches, which removes both the colour cast and any flare offset. With a grey card each channel is scaled so the card reads 18%. The corrected colour is converted to CIELAB (D65).

    Chips are the Munsell soil-book chips (5R to 5Y, the gley pages and neutrals), converted from the Munsell renotation data (illuminant C) to CIELAB under D65. The tool draws 1,500 plausible colours from the sampling error of the soil and card patches plus the residual error set above, assigns each draw to its nearest chip by CIEDE2000, and reports the share of draws per chip. Value is also computed directly from luminance with the ASTM D1535 relation. The probabilities are model-derived: they are only as good as the residual error you set, and are not a substitute for checking against a physical chart.
